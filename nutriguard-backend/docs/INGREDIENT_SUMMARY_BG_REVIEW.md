Sulphite ammonia caramel (E 150d), also called caramel colour class IV, is a complex mixture of compounds, not a single substance. 4-Methylimidazole (4-MEI) is a separate substance that forms as a by-product when caramel colours are made with ammonia compounds (classes III and IV). It is not the colour itself. EU rules limit its content in E 150d (see the EU note below).

### EFFECTS

EN:

For the colour itself, EFSA's 2011 re-evaluation of the caramel colours (E 150a-d) had found that estimated intake could exceed the acceptable daily intake (ADI) for E 150a, E 150c and E 150d. A refined exposure assessment in 2012, using updated industry use levels, found intake considerably lower and found that the group ADI of 300 mg per kg of body weight per day for the four caramel colours combined was not exceeded for any population group. Only E 150c, a different caramel colour with its own lower ADI (100 mg per kg of body weight per day), could still be exceeded by toddlers and adults.

The cancer concern about 4-MEI comes from animal feeding studies of the pure substance, not from studies of E 150d itself. In the US National Toxicology Program's 2-year feeding studies (2007), 4-MEI at about 40 to 170 mg per kg of body weight per day increased lung tumours (alveolar/bronchiolar adenoma and carcinoma) in male and female mice, which the NTP rated as clear evidence. In female rats, at about 60 to 260 mg per kg per day, the evidence was equivocal (mononuclear cell leukaemia, significantly increased in the highest-dose group). In male rats, at about 30 to 115 mg per kg per day, there was no evidence. The NTP notes that determining the actual risk to humans requires a wider analysis than these studies. The International Agency for Research on Cancer lists 4-MEI as Group 2B, possibly carcinogenic to humans (Monographs Volume 101, 2013).

In the NTP tests 4-MEI was not mutagenic in Salmonella and did not raise micronucleus frequencies in rat or mouse bone marrow or in mouse blood. Regulators have read this differently: a 2016 review reports that EFSA judged 4-MEI not genotoxic and used a no-observed-adverse-effect level of 80 mg per kg per day, whereas California's OEHHA judged the testing not comprehensive enough to rule out a genotoxic mode of action.

This summary cites no human study of E 150d or 4-MEI and cancer, so the cancer findings above are animal findings. Whether they matter for people depends on dose: the FDA states that the NTP studies used levels that far exceed current estimates of human exposure to 4-MEI from food. Two 2025 papers show that the discussion continues (abstracts only were read). A risk assessment by Scialli (Regulatory Toxicology and Pharmacology) combined NTP rat reproductive-study data with FDA 90th-percentile US dietary exposure, reported margins of exposure of 735 to 1,489 (100 or more is considered low concern) and concluded that reproductive or developmental risk from US dietary 4-MEI is not expected. A review by Shikha and colleagues (Toxicology Letters) summarises reports from experimental models of liver, nervous-system and reproductive effects, notes that standard mutagenicity assays are largely negative, and states that emerging studies link 4-MEI to disruptions in glucose and lipid metabolism, which it says raises concerns about chronic low-dose dietary exposure; its abstract gives no doses or study designs for these. Neither paper is a human study or a regulatory decision.
